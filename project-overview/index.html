Project Overview

We are working with four key communities in the sample data ecosystem. Click on a community below to learn about our work.

Sample Repositories

Landscape Survey of U.S. Earth and Space Science Sample Repositories

In the first half of Y1, the PI team will conduct a statistically representative survey of U.S.-based Earth and space science sample repositories (n≈300) to assess current use and perceptions of sample PIDs and roadblocks to uptake. The survey will ask participants about their awareness and use of PIDs in repositories, about their challenges using PIDs, and how they want to show the impact of their collections. This study will allow us to identify functional, technical, or social roadblocks to PID uptake and discern where technical developments (from commercial collections management software, PID minting and management systems) can support sample repositories in making PIDs useful for their needs. As holders of the U.S. national collections, buy-in from sample repositories is critical to the health of the sample data ecosystem. The results of the survey will be published in a peer-reviewed journal and the PI’s will present results in virtual venues and at conferences of relevance (e.g., annual meeting of U.S. marine and lacustrine sample curators (in person), ESIP Physical Sample Curation Cluster (virtual), U.S. Geological Survey NGGDPP annual meeting (virtual).

Case Studies to Understand Repository Realities and Workflows

Complementing this work, in Year 1, the UA team will develop descriptive case studies at three sample repositories. These case studies will deepen our understanding of roadblocks to and opportunities for PID adoption in repositories. Case studies are a valuable and effective way of understanding sociotechnical phenomena in context; they can reveal social dynamics, workflow quirks, administrative obstacles and other important dynamics that surveys alone cannot reveal. A holistic approach for selecting the three sites will aim to maximize capturing variation with respect to the following criteria: (1) collection size, (2) funding source(s), (3) staff size, and (4) disciplinary focus. Site visits will be conducted over four days and will involve individual interviews with repository staff, observations of curatorial activities, and group discussions. Insights from the site visits will result in a peer-reviewed publication and will be disseminated broadly.

Drawing the Sample Repository Roadmap

In Year 2, following completion of the survey and site visits, the UA team will draft a community roadmap summarizing outcomes and insight derived from the work, including key needs (e.g., technical, fiscal, resource/guidance) and community visions for leveraging sample PIDs. Participants from the initial survey will be invited to provide feedback on the roadmap during an eight-week comment period, via a series of virtual small group discussions hosted on Zoom, and through a simple Google form on the website. Other community-members who did not participate in the survey but also wish to provide comments will also be invited. All contributors providing feedback via either of the mechanisms will be invited to be acknowledged or elevated as contributors/co-authors on the final roadmap, as appropriate.

Researchers

Improving Research Data Management Workflows for Samples through Participatory Action

We will engage 35 researchers in a four-semester cohort-based program to train them in open science practices related to FAIR sample data and sample stewardship. Over the duration of the program, cohort members will become champions for FAIR sample data stewardship, progressively infusing what they learn in their day-to-day roles as mentors, collaborators, educators, data producers, authors, and beyond. We will draw on methods from Participatory Action Research (PAR) to guide our work and structure our program; activities will be iterative and goals co-designed with participants. In Year 1, the UA team will develop applicant criteria (with input and review from the Community Advisory Panel) and a short application process. Participation will be open to researchers in any career stage and country. Calls soliciting applications will be made widely via disciplinary listservs, academic society online forums, and Slack and other social media channels. In selecting participants, effort will be made to ensure the cohort captures a range of sub-disciplines, and where possible, career stage and location. To broaden participation, we will support entirely virtual participation for up to ten researchers. The cohort will be selected and notified by the end of Year 1. Participants will receive a stipend for their participation in the program. The program will officially begin in Year 2 with an initial two-day kickoff workshop at the University of Arizona.

Drawing the Researcher Roadmap

In the final semester of the program, participants will begin drafting the research community roadmap; the project team will continue developing this after the program’s end. The roadmap will contain: (1) executive summary of the researcher program, (2) key suggested points of opportunity for researchers to integrate sample and sample data management education into their activities, (3) an outline of future needs, including desired CI tools to support researchers in managing their sample metadata and using PIDs, and (4) incentives and other structural support needed.

Scholarly Publishers

Assessing the State of Sample Citation in ESS Scholarly Publishing

In Year 2, we will conduct a landscape assessment (via content analysis and text mining) of current scholarly journal sample citation guidance and practices. We will select a subset of scholarly journals that contain sample-based research and (1) identify whether they have any author guidance on sample citation and whether it aligns with leading community practices and (2) for a scoped publication period for these journals, quantify the types, usage, resolvability, and placement of sample PIDs within journal articles. Outcomes from this assessment will result in a peer-reviewed publication and will be socialized amongst the scholarly publishing community and at ESIP, European Geophysical Union (EGU), and AGU.

Focus Groups about Supporting Sample PIDs in Scholarly Publications

Grounded in the results of the landscape analysis, the UA team will conduct focus groups with editors and publishers in Earth and space sciences, employing the Nominal Group Technique, which has previously been used in similar CI projects to derive expert consensus on topics. The UA team will prepare focus group questions, consulting with Community Advisory Panel members who have experience engaging with the publishing community for their feedback. Calls for participation will be circulated amongst journal and editor-relevant listservs and social networks, including Coalition for Publishing Data in the Earth and Space Sciences (COPDESS). With their permission, participating editors and journals will be acknowledged in the synthesis output for this work. Results from this work will be circulated back to the venues engaged for the call for participation and disseminated through the Advisory Panel’s networks, where relevant.

Drawing the Scholarly Publishing Roadmap

The roadmap for scholarly publishers will: (1) provide background and context relevant to publishers about the sample data ecosystem, including an overview of the previously published outcomes of the survey, (2) provide a synthesis of the interviews and discussions with editors and publishers, and (3) comment on pathways for moving forward in supporting sample PIDs in scholarly publications. The scholarly publisher roadmap will also be presented in key publisher and editor forums and conferences.

Data Repositories

Data Repository-Focused Webinar Series

In Year 1, a webinar series jointly hosted through ESIP by the Physical Sample Curation Cluster and the Council of Data Facilities will engage data repositories in education about sample PIDs and showcase early adopters of sample-PID infrastructure. The series will consist of five presentations from sample PID infrastructure organizations and data repository leaders/software developers. The webinar series will serve to provide a space for data repositories to learn about the approaches their peers are taking to integrate sample PID infrastructure and discuss common challenges and needs with PID infrastructure providers. Discussion during webinars will be used to refine the agenda and approach for hackathons in Year 2. The webinar series will be announced on relevant listservs, social channels, and via the project website.

Hybrid-Format Hackathons

In Year 1, a hybrid-format hackathon for data repositories will be hosted to drive focused work on implementation of sample PID infrastructure. The two-day hackathon will be co-located with an annual in person meeting, and will support 12 in-person attendees and additional attendees via Zoom.In Year 2, a second, entirely-virtual hackathon will run as a series of four half-day sessions over two weeks. This hackathon will adopt a similar structure to the first hackathon, modified to accommodate the four-day structure and to leverage collaborative online modalities (e.g., Zoom breakout rooms, collaborative tools such as Slido, Miro Board). Reports from both hackathons will be made openly available on the project website and via the project’s Zenodo community.

Drawing the Data Repository Roadmap

The UA team will draft an initial community roadmap summarizing outcomes and insight derived from the activities conducted in and community visions for leveraging sample PIDs. Participants will be invited to provide feedback on the roadmap during an eight-week comment period, via small group discussions on Zoom and a Google form. All contributors providing feedback via either of the mechanisms will be invited to be acknowledged or elevated as contributors/co-authors on the final roadmap, as appropriate.
